Homesick in College? Why It Happens and What Helps

By Mentally Prepare · 7 October 2026

You were looking forward to this. New city, your own room, your own schedule. And now it's the second week and you're lying awake thinking about the sound of your family's kitchen in the evening.

Maybe it shows up as a heaviness after every call home. Maybe it's a strange irritation with everything here: the food, the noise, the way people talk. Maybe it's just a quiet wish to be somewhere that already knows you.

That's homesickness. It's one of the most common parts of starting college or university, and it doesn't mean you made the wrong choice.

Why homesickness hits harder than you expected

Homesickness is less about missing a building and more about missing what home did for you without you noticing. At home, a lot of things were already taken care of:

  • Routine. Meals, sleep and the shape of the day were mostly decided for you.
  • Being known. People around you already understood your moods without an explanation.
  • Small comforts. Familiar food, your own bed, a language or accent everyone shared.
  • A place in the story. You had a role: the sibling, the friend, the one who always did a certain thing.

College removes most of these at once, usually during the same weeks you're meeting new people, learning a new system and trying to look like you're coping. That combination is why it can feel bigger than you thought it would.

It can also arrive late. Some students feel fine for the first few weeks and then get hit after a visit home, around exams, or during a festival they'd normally spend with family.

Homesick or lonely? They overlap, but they're different

Homesickness points backwards: you miss specific people, places and routines. Loneliness is about the present: the gap between the connection you want here and the connection you feel.

You can feel one without the other. You might have new friends and still miss home terribly. Or you might not miss home at all and still feel alone. If loneliness is the bigger part for you, our guide to feeling lonely in college goes deeper into that.

6 small things that help with homesickness

None of these make homesickness disappear overnight. They make the days a little more manageable while the new place slowly becomes familiar.

1. Build one anchor into your day

Home felt steady partly because the day had a shape. Pick one small thing you do at the same time each day: morning chai at the same stall, a walk after the last class, ten minutes of reading before bed. A single fixed point gives the rest of the day something to hang on.

2. Plan calls home instead of reaching for them constantly

Talking to family can help. But if every spare moment goes to calls or messages with home, you might find less room to settle where you are. Try agreeing on a few regular times to talk, and let the hours in between belong to your new life. If calls leave you feeling worse, a shorter call can still be a good call.

3. Bring a small piece of home with you

A photo, a blanket, a particular snack, a playlist from home. Familiar things in an unfamiliar room aren't childish. They're a bridge.

4. Find "your" place on campus

A corner of the library, a bench, a café table you return to. Having one spot that feels like yours makes a large campus feel smaller and more yours.

5. Eat something that tastes like home

Food carries memory. Find a place nearby that makes something close to what you grew up eating, or ask for a simple recipe from home and cook it with a roommate. Sharing it is also an easy way to start a conversation.

6. Tell one person how you actually feel

Homesickness often comes with a sense that everyone else is fine. Many of them aren't. Saying "I've been missing home more than I expected" to a roommate, a classmate or a counsellor can make it feel a lot less like a private failure.

What to do on the hardest evenings

Homesickness is often strongest at night, when the day's distractions stop. On those evenings:

  • Keep the goal small: get through tonight, not the whole semester.
  • Do one comforting, ordinary thing: a shower, a familiar show, a warm drink.
  • Write down what you miss most today. Naming it can loosen its grip a little.
  • Message someone, even briefly. It doesn't have to be a deep conversation.

If writing helps you, our guide on how to start journaling has simple prompts for nights like this.

Will it pass?

For many students, homesickness eases as the new place builds its own routines and people. It doesn't usually vanish on a fixed date, and it can come back for a while around holidays or difficult weeks. That's normal too.

Missing home isn't a sign that you don't belong here. It's a sign that home mattered, and that you're in the middle of building a second place that matters.

When to reach out for more support

Homesickness is common and usually eases with time. But if it isn't easing, or if you're struggling to sleep, eat, attend classes or enjoy anything at all, it's worth talking to your college counsellor, a doctor or a qualified mental-health professional. You don't need to wait until it feels unbearable.

In India, Tele-MANAS offers round-the-clock mental-health support through the toll-free number 14416. You can find the service details on the Government of India's National Mental Health Programme page.

Outside India, Find A Helpline can help you locate support in your country. Our safety page lists more helplines.

If you feel you may harm yourself or cannot stay safe, seek urgent help through local emergency services or the nearest emergency department. Tell someone you trust and ask them to stay with you while you get help.

You moved somewhere new and it's hard. That's allowed. Tonight, one small comfort and one short message are enough.
