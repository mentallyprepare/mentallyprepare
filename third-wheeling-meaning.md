# Third wheeling meaning: what it means and what to do

If you’ve spent time with a couple or two close friends and felt like the extra person, you’ve experienced third wheeling. It can be a harmless way to describe tagging along, but it can also feel lonely. You can ask for a different kind of time together, speak up about feeling left out, or choose to spend time elsewhere.

## Third wheel meaning

A third wheel is a person who feels unwanted or unnecessary when spending time with two other people. The phrase usually describes someone accompanying a couple, though it also applies when two friends in a trio are closer to each other.

The image behind the phrase is an extra wheel: a bicycle needs two wheels, while a carriage has four. The expression dates back to the 17th century. “Fifth wheel” has a similar meaning, although “third wheel” is more specific to being with a couple.

You can call someone a third wheel, say they are “third-wheeling,” or say they “third-wheeled” in the past.

## Third wheeling meaning

Third wheeling means being the third wheel during an outing or another social situation. You might use it when a friend invites you out with their partner:

“Do you want to come out with me and my boyfriend tonight?”

“I don’t want to be a third wheel. You two should have a nice night together.”

People also use “to third-wheel” as a verb, as in “I third-wheeled on their date.”

## 3rd wheeling meaning

“3rd wheeling” means the same thing as “third wheeling.” It’s simply a shorter way to write it. You might also see “3rd wheel” in place of “third wheel.”

## Why do I feel like I'm third wheeling?

You may feel like a third wheel when the other two people share a closer bond and you’re left outside it. They may not mean to exclude you, but that doesn’t make the feeling any less uncomfortable.

A new relationship can change the time you spend with a friend. If they and their partner focus on each other, you might feel like you’re intruding. The same thing can happen in a friendship trio when two people have their own routines and conversations.

Feeling this way is common. It isn’t a personal failing, though the phrase itself can make you feel like you’re unnecessary. For more on that feeling, read [Feeling Left Out: Why It Hurts So Much and What Actually Helps](https://mymentallyprepare.com/feeling-left-out/).

## Is it okay to be a third wheel in a relationship?

Yes. Spending time with a couple can be enjoyable when everyone includes you. You might see a different side of your friend, become friends with their partner, or meet someone through their social circle.

Pay attention to how you feel during and after the time together. If you consistently feel uncomfortable or unwanted, tell your friend or step back. Social dynamics change, and you can make room for different plans as they do.

## What to do if you are a third wheel

Start by asking yourself whether you actually want to go. If you’re looking forward to seeing both people, accept the invitation and enjoy it. If you’re dreading the evening, it’s okay to decline.

When you do want to spend time together, suggest an activity based on something all three of you enjoy. You can take the lead in planning it or invite other people along. Getting to know your friend’s partner individually can make their company feel more natural, too.

If the couple becomes overly affectionate and you feel awkward, give them some space or excuse yourself. You don’t have to stay through a situation that makes you uncomfortable.

It’s also reasonable to ask your friend for one-on-one time. Try saying, “I miss hanging out with just you. Can we make plans together soon?” If you’ve been feeling excluded, say so plainly. Your friend may not have realized.
